Welcome & Purpose

Welcome to Fall_2026.DAT.5668 — Agentic AI: Autonomous Decision-Making!

In this course, you will learn how to design and apply AI agents that perceive information, reason about goals, plan a sequence of actions, and carry out decisions in business settings.

Agentic AI: Autonomous Decision-Making is an area of artificial intelligence in which systems pursue goals by combining language models with planning, tool use, memory, and feedback. These agents work with unstructured data—including text, customer reviews, documents, images, and video—and take a series of actions toward an objective. Organizations use agentic systems for customer operations, workflow automation, resource and inventory decisions, and dynamic pricing. Leading organizations increasingly expect business analysts and managers to design, supervise, and evaluate these systems when they solve practical problems.

This course is a 1.5-credit elective offered to MS-Business Analytics (MSA) students in all tracks and to other graduate students. It lays the foundation of agentic AI and teaches you how to build goal-directed agents for business decisions. The course goes into sufficient technical depth on perception, reasoning, planning, tool use, memory, multi-agent coordination, and evaluation. The emphasis is on applying these methods to practical problems.

Course Objectives

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

  • Explain how an AI agent acts through interaction with data, tools, and its environment.
  • Describe a computational approach to goal-directed behavior: perception, reasoning, planning, action, and reflection.
  • Identify the core concepts of agentic AI, including goals, tools, memory, orchestration, and human oversight.
  • Combine language models with tools and workflows so an agent can complete multi-step business tasks.
  • Apply autonomous and human-in-the-loop decision-making to business problems that use unstructured data.
  • Design, run, and evaluate an agent that solves a sequential business decision problem, such as resource allocation, operations, or pricing.
